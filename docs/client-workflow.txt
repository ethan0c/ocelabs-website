Internal. Not sent to clients. The emails in Part 3 are the client-facing pieces.


PART 1: THE TOOLS

Four tools, three of them new. Nothing here needs a paid plan to start.

Inbox (have it)
hello@ocelabs.xyz. Every client email goes from this address, never a personal one. Save the Part 3 emails as templates or snippets in the mail client so each one is a paste and a two-minute edit.

Stripe (have it)
Use Stripe Invoicing for every invoice. It sends a branded invoice by email with a pay link, produces a PDF and a receipt, sends its own overdue reminders, and pays out to Relay. No separate invoicing tool is needed. One-time setup:
• Branding: logo, brand colour, public business name OCE Labs, support email hello@ocelabs.xyz
• Statement descriptor: OCE LABS, so the charge is recognisable on the client's statement
• Turn on ACH bank payment for invoices. Card fees are a percentage of the invoice; ACH is a fraction of a percent with a low cap. On a $6,000 invoice that is roughly $175 against $5. Check the current Stripe pricing page for exact numbers.
• Invoice footer: the legal name line (see Part 4) and "Questions? hello@ocelabs.xyz"
• Invoice numbering prefix: OCE
• The monthly retainer is a Stripe subscription, not a repeated manual invoice
• Put a restricted API key (Invoices: write, Customers: write) in Railway as STRIPE_SECRET_KEY. The /pricing estimator uses it to draft each payment of a quote as an invoice. Drafts only: every invoice is still read over and sent from the Stripe dashboard.
• Put the legal name in Railway as OCE_LEGAL_NAME. It appears in the Parties section and the footer of every proposal.

Scheduling (new): Cal.com, free plan
Connect it to the calendar on the business inbox. Create two event types:
• Intro call, 30 minutes, video. Booking form asks: business name, current site if any, and what they want out of the call.
• Project review, 45 minutes, for existing clients only. Not listed publicly.
Settings: 24 hours minimum notice, 15 minutes buffer either side, a fixed window of bookable hours, automatic confirmation and reminder emails. Calendly's free plan does the same job if preferred; pick one and do not change it.

E-signature (new): any free tier
The proposal is signed before any money moves. SignWell, Documenso, and Dropbox Sign all have a free tier that covers a few documents a month, which is more than enough. Check the current limits before choosing.

Pipeline tracker
One table in Notion or Google Sheets. One row per lead. Columns: Name, Company, Email, Source, Package, Quote, Stage, Next action, Next action date. "Quote" is the /pricing link: the estimator keeps the whole quote in its URL, so paste that and the form, the proposal, and the invoices all come back from it. Stages match Part 2: New, Call booked, Recap sent, Proposal sent, Signed, Kickoff, Building, Review, Launched, Closed lost. Look at it every Monday and every Friday. If "Next action date" is in the past, that is the job for the day.


PART 2: THE STAGES

Each stage has one trigger, one thing we send, and one thing we wait for.


Stage 1: Inquiry
Trigger: the contact form lands in the inbox.
We do: reply within one business day with Email A (scheduling link). Add the row to the tracker.
A personal reply that mentions what they wrote reads as more professional than an autoresponder, and it takes two minutes from the template.
No booking after 3 business days: send Email A2 (nudge). No reply 7 days after that: mark Closed lost. No third email.


Stage 2: Intro call
Trigger: they book.
Before the call (10 minutes): read their message again, open their current site, look at two competitors, and have a likely package in mind.
On the call (30 minutes):
• 5 min: who we are, how the call will go
• 15 min: their business, who the site is for, what a visitor should do, what is wrong with what they have now, deadline, who approves
• 5 min: the likely package and its price range, said out loud. Never leave a first call without a range on the table; it filters out mismatches before we spend time on a proposal.
• 5 min: the next steps, in order, with dates
Do not quote a fixed price on the call.


Stage 3: Recap
Trigger: the call ends.
We do: send Email B the same day, with the questionnaire (client-onboarding doc) attached.
We wait for: the questionnaire. Nudge once after 5 business days.


Stage 4: Proposal
Trigger: the questionnaire comes back.
We do, within 2 business days:
• Build the quote in the /pricing estimator. Fill in the client's legal business name and email; both go on the paperwork.
• Press "Open proposal". It renders the Part 4 agreement from the quote: scope, fixed price, payment schedule, timeline, terms. Press "Download PDF".
• Upload the PDF to the e-sign tool and send it, with Email C. Paste the estimator link into the tracker's Quote column.
The proposal is valid for 14 days. Nudge at day 5 and day 12. After day 14 the price and the start date are no longer held.


Stage 5: Signature and deposit
Trigger: the signed proposal comes back.
We do, the same day: open the quote from the tracker, press "Draft in Stripe" on the Deposit line, check the draft in the dashboard and send it. Then Email D. The later payments are drafted the same way when their trigger arrives; the amounts match the signed proposal because they come from the same quote.
This is when the deposit is collected: after signature, before any work. The signed proposal says what is being bought; the deposit buys the slot in the schedule. No design work, no mockups, and no "quick look" before it clears.
Payment structure:
• Website and Brand Website: 50% deposit, 50% before launch
• Web App: 40% deposit, 30% when the design is approved, 30% before launch
• Mobile App: 30% deposit, then equal milestone payments agreed in the proposal, final payment before store submission
Deposit invoices are due on receipt. All other invoices are due in 7 days.


Stage 6: Kickoff
Trigger: all four items are in: deposit cleared, questionnaire returned, brand files shared, existing copy shared.
We do: send Email E, which confirms the kickoff date, the launch date, and the content deadline (kickoff plus 7 days). The timeline starts counting here, not before.
Create the client's shared folder, the private repository, and the staging link.


Stage 7: Build
Every Friday, send Email F: done this week, next week, what we need from you. Send it even when the answer is "nothing needed". The weekly email is the single thing that makes a small studio look like a well-run one.
Two review points, each with two rounds of revisions included:
• Design review: direction and the key pages, before development
• Staging review: the full site on a private link, before launch
Feedback is collected in one email or one document per round, from the one approver named in the questionnaire.
Anything outside the signed scope gets a change order before work starts: one paragraph describing it, a price, and the effect on the launch date. The client replies "approved" in writing. It is added to the final invoice.


Stage 8: Launch
Trigger: the client approves the staging site in writing.
We do: send the final invoice with Email G. The site goes live when it is paid. Domain, repository access, and hosting transfer all happen after the final payment, never before.


Stage 9: Handover
Trigger: the site is live.
We do: send Email H with everything listed under "What you get at handover" in the client doc. The 30 days of fixes start on this date. Put the day-30 date in the tracker.


Stage 10: Day 30
We do: send Email I. Check in, offer the retainer, and ask for a testimonial and a referral. Add the project to /work if the client agrees.

PART 4: THE PROPOSAL AND THE PAPERWORK

The proposal is one PDF, two to three pages, and it is also the contract. It is generated at /pricing/proposal from the estimator's quote, so the numbers cannot drift from the calculation; edit the wording in app/pricing/proposal/page.tsx. Sections:

1. Parties. [Legal name], doing business as OCE Labs, and the client's legal business name.
2. Scope. The package, the pages, the add-ons, pasted from the estimator summary. Then a line: "Anything not listed here is outside the scope and is quoted separately."
3. Price and payment schedule. The fixed total, each payment, and what triggers it.
4. Timeline. Weeks from kickoff, what kickoff requires, and the content deadline rule.
5. Revisions. Two rounds at each of the two review points. One named approver.
6. Changes. Written change order, approved before work starts.
7. Ownership. The client owns the site and the code once the final invoice is paid. We may show the work in our portfolio unless they ask us not to.
8. Cancellation. Either side can stop with written notice. The deposit is non-refundable once work has started. Work completed beyond the deposit is billed.
9. Late payment. Work pauses if an invoice is more than 7 days overdue, and the launch date moves by the length of the pause.
10. After launch. 30 days of fixes. The retainer is optional and separate.
11. Validity. 14 days from the date sent.
12. Signatures.

Have a lawyer read the template once. It is a one-time cost and it covers every project after.

Sole proprietor details
• Contracts and invoices name the legal person: "[Legal name], doing business as OCE Labs". The brand goes on everything; the legal name goes in the Parties section and the invoice footer.
• Confirm the DBA (trade name) is registered with the state or county. Banks and larger clients can ask for it.
• US business clients may ask for a W-9 before paying. Keep a completed one ready as a PDF. Using an EIN instead of a Social Security number on it is free to set up with the IRS.
• Whether web design is subject to sales tax depends on the state. Check once, and turn on Stripe Tax if it applies.
• Every dollar goes Stripe to Relay. Nothing is paid to a personal account or by a personal payment app.

Files per client
One shared folder per client: 01 Questionnaire, 02 Proposal (signed), 03 Brand files, 04 Copy, 05 Handover. The same structure every time, so anything can be found in a year.
